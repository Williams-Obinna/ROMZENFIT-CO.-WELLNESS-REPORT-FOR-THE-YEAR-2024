Objective of the Project

The analyses focus on identifying key wellness trends among different demographics and occupational groups. The study aims to highlight factors influencing stress levels, sleep quality, and physical activity, providing actionable insights for improving workplace and personal wellness.


IN-ANALYSIS

1. The chart shows a peak sleep duration sum around Nurses (515.6 hours) and a sharp dip around managers (6.9 hours).

2. Males have a higher total stress level (1,149) compared to females (865).

3. Individuals with a normal BMI category have the highest quality of sleep (1,494), followed by overweight individuals (1,021).

4. Age group of 41–50 has the highest stress level of 797 and age group of 21–30 have the lowest stress level of 219.

5. Doctors and nurses have the highest stress level at 478 and 405, while teachers have the lowest at 181.

6. Blood pressure range 130/85 have the highest quality of sleep at 717, while blood pressure range 125/82 has the lowest quality of sleep at 28.

7. 41–50 age group has the highest physical activity level at 8,847, and 21–30 age group at 1,747.

8. Normal BMI has the highest daily steps at 1,343,00, while the obese category has the lowest at 33,500.


Recommendations:

1. The healthcare sector (Nurses, Doctors) shows a mix of high and low sleep durations, further analysis could explore whether nurses with fixed shifts sleep more than those with rotating schedules.

2. Males may be experiencing higher stress due to workplace pressure, responsibilities, or societal expectations.

3. Excess weight may contribute to sleep disturbances, possibly due to conditions like sleep aponia.

4. The 21–30 age group has the lowest stress level of 219, indicating that younger individuals may experience less stress level compared to those in their 30s and 40s, potentially reflecting different life stages with fewer stressors.

5. Doctors and nurses have the highest stress level at 473 and 405 respectively, indicating that healthcare related occupations may experience the most significant stress burden.

6. The highest quality of sleep is observed in individuals with a blood pressure of 130/85 (717), suggesting that individuals within this range might experience better sleep, possibly due to stable cardiovascular function.

7. The 21–30 age group has the lowest physical activity level at 1,747, which is unexpected given that younger individuals are generally perceived as more active. This may indicate lifestyle shifts, such as increased screen time or reduced engagement in structured physical activities.

8. The Obese category has the lowest step count at only 33,500, indicating a potential link between excess weight and reduced mobility. This could be due to physical discomfort or joint issues.

CONCLUSION

Key learning:

· Doctors and Nurses experience the highest stress levels, indicating the need for targeted mental health programs.

· Managers have the shortest sleep duration, highlighting potential work-life balance issues.

· The 41–50 age group is the most physically active, suggesting increased health awareness with age.

· Individuals with normal BMI report better sleep quality and higher step counts, emphasizing the importance if maintaining a healthy weight.
